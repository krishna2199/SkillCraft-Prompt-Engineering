# Task 1 — Writing Better Prompts: Comparison Report

## Objective (from the official brief)

> Experiment with clarity, specificity, and tone. Take 3 vague or ineffective prompts and
> rewrite them into improved versions. Compare and analyze the outputs to understand how
> prompt design affects LLM behavior.

## Evidence used

| Source | What it is | Model and settings |
|---|---|---|
| [`results/experiment*_output.md`](../results/) | The **originally recorded** responses, one per prompt. Kept unedited. | Not recorded at the time (a chat assistant; model, date, and settings unknown) |
| [`results/rerun_qwen2.5-3b/`](../results/rerun_qwen2.5-3b/) | A **logged rerun** of the same six prompts | `qwen2.5:3b` via Ollama, temperature 0.7, seed 42 ([run_log.json](../results/rerun_qwen2.5-3b/run_log.json)) |
| [`constraint_checks_recorded.md`](constraint_checks_recorded.md), [`constraint_checks_rerun.md`](constraint_checks_rerun.md) | Automatic checks of every explicit constraint | Generated by [`../check_constraints.py`](../check_constraints.py) |

Each prompt was run once per model. This is a comparison of individual responses, not a
controlled or statistical experiment.

## How the prompts were improved

| Technique (brief) | Experiment 1: email | Experiment 2: binary search | Experiment 3: social post |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Clarity**: a defined role and task | "professional communication assistant", "formal email to a company's HR manager" | "computer science tutor for beginners" | "social media copywriter for a small business" |
| **Specificity**: audience, content, limits | prompt-engineering internship; required parts; 120–160 words | sorted array `[2, 5, 8, 12, 16, 23, 38]`, target 16; steps, pseudocode, complexity, sorted-input note | LinkedIn; small-business owners; hook, 2 benefits, CTA; 100–140 words; ≤ 3 hashtags |
| **Tone and safety** | formal; "do not invent qualifications" | "simple English" | credible tone; no fabricated statistics or testimonials |

## Rating scale

Each criterion is rated 1–5 by the reviewer: 1 = poor, 3 = acceptable with clear gaps,
5 = fully meets the goal. Each rating has a written reason. Constraint compliance is
measured automatically, not rated. The [methodology](../../docs/methodology.md) explains who
assigned these ratings.

## Experiment 1 — Internship email

**Original prompt:** `Write an email asking for an internship.`
**Improved prompt:** [`experiment1_improved.txt`](../prompts/experiment1_improved.txt)

### Recorded outputs

| Criterion | Original | Improved | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relevance | 3 | 5 | The original is a generic internship request. The improved one names prompt engineering and explains the interest in prompting. |
| Clarity | 4 | 4 | Both are readable and polite. |
| Structure | 4 | 3 | The original has a full structure. The improved one **has no subject line**, although the prompt required one. |
| Usefulness | 2 | 4 | The original claims "Please find my resume attached" and leaves three `[Your …]` placeholders. The improved one offers to share a resume instead. |

| Constraint (improved prompt) | Result |
|---|---|
| 120–160 words | PASS (146 words) |
| Subject line | **FAIL** (missing) |
| No invented qualifications | **Cannot be verified.** Both outputs state a name and degree ("Electronics at IIT Madras") that are **not in either prompt**. They must have come from the chat assistant's conversation context or personalisation, not from the prompt. |

### Logged rerun (`qwen2.5:3b`)

- **Original:** a template with 16 bracketed placeholders, such as `[Your Company]` and
  `[mention a specific reason…]`. The vague prompt gave the model nothing concrete to say.
- **Improved:** it includes the subject line that the recorded output missed. However, it has
  **100 words (FAIL, below 120)**, no greeting line, and a `[Your Name]` placeholder.

**Finding:** specifying the audience and role clearly focused both models. Neither model met
every structural constraint, which shows why each constraint must be checked rather than
assumed.

## Experiment 2 — Binary search explanation

**Original prompt:** `Explain binary search.`
**Improved prompt:** [`experiment2_improved.txt`](../prompts/experiment2_improved.txt)

### Recorded outputs

| Criterion | Original | Improved | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relevance | 4 | 5 | Both explain binary search. The improved one targets beginners with an analogy. |
| Clarity | 3 | 4 | The original's example finds 40 on the first comparison, so it never shows halving. The improved one shows three steps and explains why each half is discarded. |
| Structure | 3 | 4 | The improved one has numbered sections, pseudocode, and a complexity table. However, its array/index diagram was flattened when copied from the chat interface (`2 0 5 1 8 2 …`), and its LaTeX (`\(O(\log n)\)`) is not rendered. |
| Usefulness | 3 | 5 | The improved one covers all five requested elements: steps, why halving works, pseudocode, complexity, and the sorted-input requirement. |

| Correctness check (automatic) | Result |
|---|---|
| Improved: example array is sorted | PASS (`[2, 5, 8, 12, 16, 23, 38]`) |
| Improved: worked steps mid = 3 → 5 → 4, found at index 4 | PASS (matches a reference implementation) |
| Improved: "about 10 comparisons for 1,000 elements" | PASS (⌊log₂ 1000⌋ + 1 = 10) |
| Original: printed result `4` for `binary_search(numbers, 50)` | PASS |
| Original: prose example and code use the same target | **FAIL** (the prose searches for 40, the code for 50) |

### Logged rerun (`qwen2.5:3b`)

- **Original:** the explanation of the algorithm is correct, but the worked example goes wrong
  at step 6. It recomputes `middle = (0 + 5) // 2` after `low` had already moved. It then
  **loops on the same step until it is cut off at 2,048 tokens**.
- **Improved:** the first two steps are correct (mid 3 → 12, mid 5 → 23). After that it
  **repeats the same two comparisons nine times**, using an invalid `(4 + 3) // 2`, before
  stating the correct answer (index 4). The pseudocode and the O(log n) complexity are
  correct.

**Finding:** the improved prompt reliably produced every requested section with both models.
However, a more specific prompt **does not fix a model's reasoning errors**: the 3B model's
traces were wrong in both versions. Worked examples must be checked against a reference, as
`check_constraints.py` does.

## Experiment 3 — Business social media post

**Original prompt:** `Make a social media post for my business.`
**Improved prompt:** [`experiment3_improved.txt`](../prompts/experiment3_improved.txt)

### Recorded outputs

| Criterion | Original | Improved | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relevance | 3 | 5 | The original lists four services for an unspecified audience. The improved one targets small-business owners who do repetitive work. |
| Clarity | 3 | 5 | The improved one has one message: save time on repetitive work. |
| Structure | 3 | 5 | The improved one has a hook, two benefits, and a call to action, exactly as requested. |
| Usefulness | 3 | 4 | The improved one is ready to post on LinkedIn. The original needs trimming (7 hashtags, many emoji). |

| Constraint (improved prompt) | Result |
|---|---|
| 100–140 words | PASS (125 words, hashtags excluded) |
| At most 3 hashtags | PASS (3) |
| No fabricated statistics or testimonials | PASS |

Both recorded posts use the brand name **"ATObrks"**, which is **not in either prompt**. As in
Experiment 1, the chat assistant used details from its conversation context.

### Logged rerun (`qwen2.5:3b`)

- **Original:** the model **asked clarifying questions instead of writing a post** ("could you
  please tell me a bit more about your business…"). This is a direct, visible effect of a
  vague prompt.
- **Improved:** it produced a post that follows the theme, with 3 hashtags and no invented
  statistics. However, it has only **35 words (FAIL, below 100)**, and its hook and call to
  action are weak.

**Finding:** the improved prompt turned a request the model could not act on into a usable
post. A small model may still ignore length limits.

---

## Overall conclusions

1. **Specificity changed what the models did.** The vague prompts produced generic content,
   placeholder templates, or a clarifying question. The improved prompts produced on-topic
   responses for the intended audience in all six improved runs (3 recorded, 3 rerun).
2. **Constraints were often only partly followed.** Across both models, 5 of the 8 length and
   format checks on the improved prompts passed (word range and subject line for Experiment 1;
   word range and hashtag limit for Experiment 3). Word limits and required parts need to be
   checked after generation.
3. **Better prompts do not guarantee correct content.** The binary-search traces from the 3B
   model were wrong despite a precise prompt.
4. **Hidden context affects results.** The recorded chat outputs used a name, a university,
   and a brand that were not in any prompt. Logged runs with no prior context, like the
   rerun, are needed to judge a prompt by itself.

## Limitations

- There is one response per prompt per model, so there is no measure of run-to-run variation.
- The rerun is not perfectly reproducible on CPU. With the same seed, the improved-email rerun
  produced 128 tokens on a first run and 117 on the saved run.
- The 1–5 ratings are one reviewer's judgement. The automatic checks are objective but narrow.
